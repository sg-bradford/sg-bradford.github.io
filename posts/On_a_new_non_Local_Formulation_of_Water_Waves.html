Derivation of the Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation for Small Amplitude Deep Water Waves from a Non-Local Formulation

An expository derivation of a result shown by M. J. Ablowitz, A. S. Fokas, and Z. H. Musslimani in “On a new non-local formulation of water waves”.

The Classical Gravity Water Wave Problem

We start by defining the domain

\[ D=\{-\infty<x<\infty,\quad -h<y<\eta(x, t),\quad t>0\} \]

The classical gravity water wave problem satisfies

\[ \Delta\phi=0\quad\text{in }D, \tag{1}\] \[ \phi_y=0\quad\text{on }y=-h, \tag{2}\] \[ \eta_t+\phi_x\eta_x=\phi_y\quad\text{on }y=\eta, \tag{3}\] \[ \phi_t+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^2+g\eta=\frac{\sigma\eta_{xx}}{\rho(1+\eta_x^2)^\frac{3}{2}}\quad\text{on }y=\eta. \tag{4}\]

Deriving the Dynamic Boundary Condition

We aim to reformulate these equations purely in terms of functions on the free boundary. These functions are \(\eta(x, t)\) and \(q(x, t):=\phi(x_1, \eta(x, t), t)\).

Since Equation 3 is satisfied on the free surface, and

\[ \phi_x+\phi_y\eta_x=q_x, \tag{5}\]

we have

\[ \eta_t+[q_x-\phi_y\eta_x]\eta_x=\phi_y \] \[ \implies\phi_y=\frac{\eta_x q_x+\eta_t}{1+\eta_x^2},\quad\phi_x=\frac{q_x-\eta_x\eta_t}{1+\eta_x^2}. \]

This implies that

\[ |\nabla\phi|^2=\phi_x^2+\phi_y^2 \] \[ =\frac{[q_x^2-2\eta_x\eta_t q_x+\eta_x^2\eta_t^2]+[\eta_x^2 q_x^2+2\eta_x\eta_t q_x+\eta_t^2]}{(1+\eta_x^2)^2} \] \[ =\frac{[q_x^2+\eta_t^2]+\eta_x^2[q_x^2+\eta_t^2]}{(1+\eta_x^2)^2} \] \[ =\frac{q_x^2+\eta_t^2}{1+\eta_x^2} \]

Substituting \(|\nabla\phi|^2\) and \(\phi_t=q_t-\phi_y\eta_t\) into Equation 4, we have

\[ [q_t-\left[\frac{\eta_x q_x+\eta_t}{1+\eta_x^2}\right]\eta_t]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{q_x^2+\eta_t^2}{1+\eta_x^2}\right]+g\eta=\frac{\sigma\eta_{xx}}{\rho(1+\eta_x^2)^\frac{3}{2}} \] \[ \implies q_t+g\eta-\frac{\sigma\eta_{xx}}{\rho(1+\eta_x^2)^\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{q_x^2+\eta_t^2}{1+\eta_x^2}-\frac{2\eta_x\eta_t q_x+2\eta_t^2}{1+\eta_x^2}\right)=0 \] \[ \implies q_t+g\eta-\frac{\sigma\eta_{xx}}{\rho(1+\eta_x^2)^\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(1+\eta_x^2)q_x^2}{1+\eta_x^2}-\frac{\eta_x^2q_x^2+2\eta_x\eta_t q_x+\eta_t^2}{1+\eta_x^2}\right)=0 \] \[ \implies q_t+g\eta-\frac{\sigma\eta_{xx}}{\rho(1+\eta_x^2)^\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}q_x^2-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\eta_t+\eta_x q_x)^2}{1+\eta_x^2}=0 \] This is the dynamic boundary condition - Bernoulli’s equation.

Deriving the Global Relation

Suppose that \(\phi(x,y)\) and \(\psi(x,y)\) are harmonic functions in \(D\) - i.e. they satisfy Equation 1. Then we have the identity

\[ \phi_y(\psi_{xx}+\psi_{yy})+\psi_y(\phi_{xx}+\phi_{yy})=0. \]

Which can be rewritten as

\[ (\phi_y\psi_x+\phi_x\psi_y)_x+(\phi_y\psi_y-\phi_x\psi_x)_y=0. \tag{6}\]

Now let \(\psi(x,y)=e^{ikx+ky}\). Since \(\psi\) is harmonic, we can write

\[ [\psi(ik\phi_y+k\phi_x)]_x+[\psi(k\phi_x-ik\phi_x)]_y=0 \]

In other words, the vector field \(\vec{F}(x,y)=\psi(ik\phi_y+k\phi_x)\hat{i}+\psi(k\phi_x-ik\phi_x)\hat{j}\) is non-divergent - i.e. \(\nabla\cdot F=0\). Since \(F\) is a continuously differentiable vector field,

\[ \int_D[\nabla\cdot F]dV=\int_{\partial D}[F\cdot\hat{n}]dS \]

by the divergence theorem. Thus, we have the global relation

\[ \int_{\partial D}[e^{ikx+ky}[(ik\phi_y+k\phi_x)\hat{i}+(k\phi_y-ik\phi_x)\hat{j}]\cdot\hat{n}]dS=0 \]

Here, we make the assumption that \(\eta\) and \(|\nabla\phi|\) vanish as \(|x|\to\infty\) (the physical interpretation of this assumption is that waters are calm very far away from the waves we are observing). From this assumption, we have

\[ \int_{\partial D}[F\cdot\hat{n}]dS=\left[\int_{-\infty}^\infty[F\cdot\hat{n}]dS\right]_{y=-h}+\left[\int_{-\infty}^\infty[F\cdot\hat{n}]dS\right]_{y=\eta}=0 \]

Since Equation 2 holds for \(y=-h\), the bottom integral becomes

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty[F\cdot\hat{n}]_{y=-h}=\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx-kh}(ik\phi_x)(x,-h,t)dx \]

Since Equation 3 and Equation 5 hold for \(y=\eta\), the top integral becomes

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty[F\cdot\hat{n}]_{y=\eta}=\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx+k\eta}[-(ik\phi_y+k\phi_x)\eta_x+(k\phi_y-ik\phi_x)]dx \] \[ =\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx+k\eta}[k(\phi_y-\eta_x\phi_x)-ik(\phi_x+\eta_x\phi_y)]dx \] \[ =\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx+k\eta}[k\eta_t-ikq_x]dx. \]

Thus, we have

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx}[e^{k\eta}(k\eta_t-ikq_x)+e^{-kh}(ik\phi_x)(x,-h,t)]dx=0. \]

Factoring out \(k\) yields

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx}[e^{k\eta}(\eta_t-iq_x)+e^{-kh}(i\phi_x)(x,-h,t)]dx=0. \tag{7}\]

This global relation is not yet all that useful to us, since the term \(e^{ikx-kh}(i\phi_x)\) is evaluated at the bottom \(y=-h\). Towards the aim of removing this term, we can repeat the process above by substituting the harmonic function \(\psi(x,y)=e^{ikx-ky}\) into Equation 6 to derive a similar global relation

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx}[e^{-k\eta}(\eta_t+iq_x)-e^{kh}(i\phi_x)(x,-h,t)]dx=0. \tag{8}\]

Multiplying Equation 7 by \(e^{kh}\), Equation 8 by \(e^{-kh}\), and adding the two, we get

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx}[e^{k(\eta+h)}(\eta_t-iq_x)+e^{-k(\eta+h)}(n_t+iq_x)]dx=0. \]

Or, multiplying by \(i\),

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{ikx}[e^{k(\eta+h)}(i\eta_t+q_x)+e^{-k(\eta+h)}(in_t-q_x)]dx=0. \tag{9}\]

Interestingly, since \(\psi(x,y)=\{e^{\pm ikx\pm ky},e^{\pm ikx\mp ky}\}\) are all harmonic functions, Equation 9 is still valid if we set \(k\to-k\)

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}[e^{-k(\eta+h)}(i\eta_t+q_x)+e^{k(\eta+h)}(in_t-q_x)]dx=0. \tag{10}\]

Deriving the Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation From the Deep Water Limit

As we take \(h\to\infty\) in Equation 10, supposing \(k>0\), we get

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}e^{k\eta}e^{kh}(in_t-q_x)dx=0 \] \[ \implies\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}e^{k\eta}(in_t-q_x)dx=0. \tag{11}\]

Supposing \(k<0\), we get

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}e^{-k\eta}(i\eta_t+q_x)dx=0 \tag{12}\]

Equation 11 and Equation 12 can be written as the single equation

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}e^{|k|\eta}(i\eta_t-\text{sgn}(k)q_x)dx=0. \]

We can replace \(\eta\to\varepsilon\eta\) and \(q\to\varepsilon q\) for \(\varepsilon\ll1\) to create a small wave approximation.

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}e^{\varepsilon|k|\eta}(i\varepsilon\eta_t-\text{sgn}(k)\varepsilon q_x)dx=0 \] \[ \implies\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}e^{\varepsilon|k|\eta}(i\eta_t-\text{sgn}(k)q_x)dx=0, \]

and label \[ \varepsilon q_t+\varepsilon g\eta-\frac{\sigma\varepsilon\eta_{xx}}{\rho(1+\varepsilon^2\eta_x^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}q_x^2-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\varepsilon\eta_t+\varepsilon^2\eta_x q_x)^2}{1+\varepsilon^2\eta_x^2}=0 \] \[ \implies q_t+g\eta-\frac{\sigma\eta_{xx}}{\rho(1+\varepsilon^2\eta_x^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}q_x^2-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{(\eta_t+\varepsilon\eta_x q_x)^2}{1+\varepsilon^2\eta_x^2}=0 \]

Expanding up to \(\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)\), we have

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}(1+\varepsilon|k|\eta+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^2|k|^2\eta^2+\dots)(i\eta_t-\text{sgn}(k)q_x)dx=0, \]

and using the generalized Binomial theorem

\[ (a+x)^n|_{|x/a|<1}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(k+1)\Gamma(n-k+1)}a^{n-k}x^k, \]

we get

\[ q_t+g\eta-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}(1-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^2\eta_x^2)\eta_{xx}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}q_x^2-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}(\eta_t^2+2\varepsilon\eta_t\eta_x q_x)+\dots=0. \tag{13}\]

Differentiating Equation 13 with respect to \(x\), and denoting \(Q:=q_x\) yields

\[ \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-ikx}(1+\varepsilon|k|\eta+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^2|k|^2\eta^2+\dots)(i\eta_t-\text{sgn}(k)Q)dx=0, \]

\[ Q_t+g\eta_x-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}[\eta_{xx}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^2\eta_x^2\eta_{xx}]_x+\varepsilon QQ_x-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}[\eta_t^2+2\varepsilon\eta_t\eta_x Q]_x+\dots=0. \]

At this point, we make a quasi-monochromatic wave expansion of \(\eta\) and \(Q\).
